I’m Chu, 35, Chinese and based in Leeds. I work in early years education, hold a PhD in Business and Economics (Marketing) from the University of Leeds, and compete in men’s physique as a natural bodybuilder. I take relationships seriously, and I’m looking for my future wife. It seemed fair to say so at the start.

There’s more to me than a profile can fit, or one meeting can cover. So here’s a little more about me and, at the end, a few questions for you, because I’d like to hear about you too.

Part 1 of 4 Meet Chu

Beyond the profile

A few things I’d want you to know about me first.

  • Care, in practice

    Working with young children means paying attention, keeping routines going and communicating clearly with families. I’d like that same thoughtfulness to be part of how we look after each other.

  • A curious mind, put to good use

    My PhD at Leeds taught me to ask better questions and check the evidence. I enjoy making complicated things simpler, whether that’s planning a trip or weighing up a career move.

  • Discipline, with room for a life

    I train as a natural bodybuilder, and I competed in my first men’s physique show in July 2026. Food, conversation and time together matter to me too. I’d want us to make room for the things we each care about, rather than expecting one person to fit around the other.

  • Solid ground to build on

    I have a permanent, full-time job as an early years educator with a leading UK nursery group, and I could move to other centres. Alongside it, I do research and pursue other career opportunities. That job is my safety net, and a steady base while I build towards a life with more freedom.

Part 2 of 4 Everyday life

Ordinary days, yours and mine

Four everyday scenes we could share. In each one, what you enjoy comes first. I don’t know much about that yet, and I’d like to find out.

Pick a scene

A quiet evening

You pick dinner and what we watch, or show me something you love. My suggestions can wait for another night. Then the kind of conversation that makes us forget to press play.

Why it’s good for both of us You get to share what you love, and I get to find out what makes you happy. Films and series are among my favourite things anyway, so it’s a good night for me too.

A day out

Somewhere you’ve been wanting to go, or somewhere new to us both, like a market town or the coast. We find somewhere good to eat, and I take the photos.

Why it’s good for both of us I enjoy improving my photography and helping people look their best in photos. You’d come home with pictures of yourself you’d want to keep, and we’d both have the memories to go with them.

An idea of yours

You tell me about something you’d like to start or change. You do most of the talking, and I ask good questions and really listen.

Why it’s good for both of us You’d have someone on your side who takes your idea seriously. I’d get to understand what drives you, and that matters to me more than the plan itself.

A healthy routine

Our plans come first. I fit my training into the afternoon or straight after work, around our time together. You’re welcome to join me, and just as welcome not to.

Why it’s good for both of us It helps us both: staying fit keeps me healthy and gives me the focus to do well at work, so we can build a good future. It also keeps me ready to compete.

The small things

I’d remember a place you mentioned once, and book it. I’d plan our meet-ups so all you have to do is turn up. And I’d want you to feel free to tell me what you need.

Your ambitions have room here

If you’re working towards something and you’d ever like a hand, I’m good at research, planning and writing, and at making sense of data. That might mean comparing options or breaking a big project into manageable steps. It stays yours, with the kind of help you actually want.

A sounding board, an extra pair of hands when you want them, and on the days it isn’t about the project at all, company.

What I’m hoping we share

I’m kind, and I value honesty, affection and being able to talk about a problem calmly, without it turning into a row. I’d rather listen first and apologise when I’m wrong than let things build up.

I want a monogamous relationship and marriage with someone who wants that too. I’m open to children, depending on what my future wife wants, and if children come along, I’ll be as responsible a father as I can be. I hope we can support each other’s ambitions, keep our own interests and make the big decisions together.

My career and bodybuilding matter to me, and I want to understand what matters just as much to you: what you’d like from life, and what you need to feel happy and secure. I’d do my best to help with what you want and to make sure you have what you need. And I’d keep asking, because these things can change over time.

Part 3 of 4 Looking ahead

A future we would decide together

Three stages, in the order I’d hope to take them. None of them is mine to decide alone.

  1. Getting to know each other

    Seeing more of each other. Finding out what each of us enjoys, and whether we like each other’s company and ways of thinking.

  2. Building a partnership

    Learning how we each handle a hard week, a disagreement and good news, and finding a rhythm that works for us both.

  3. Choosing a home and family life

    If we both want it: where to live, and whether and when to have children. All at our own pace.

Where I stand

My job gives me a regular income and a settled routine. It’s a solid foundation, and I’d rather show you steady progress than sell you an illusion.

The plan is simple: keep progressing in that job, and grow the research and analytics side of my career alongside it, one step at a time, so that over the years we’d have more choice about how we live.

The UK is my preferred base, because my work and my life are established here. China is an option too, if you’d like that and we’re both comfortable with the uncertainty a move would bring. Where we live has to make sense for both our lives and careers.

What would we need to talk through?
  • Marriage and children. I’m ready for marriage and open to children, and I’d like to hear your view too, whenever you feel like talking about it.
  • Money, housing and both careers. We’d plan big steps, like a home, together and in good time. Because I could move to other centres, my job doesn’t have to decide where we live. As long as a decision is best for us as a team, I can adapt to it.
  • Backgrounds and beliefs. I’m very adaptable here, and I’d want room for both of ours.
